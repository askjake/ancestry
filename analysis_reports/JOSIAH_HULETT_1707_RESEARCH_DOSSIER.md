# Josiah Hulett / Hulet / Hewlett / Howlet (born 1707) — Research Dossier

**Research date:** 2026-09-28  
**Subject:** Josiah Howlet/Hulet/Hulett/Hewlett, born 6 July 1707 at Newton, Province of Massachusetts Bay  
**Research question:** Is this man the historical “Captain” associated with the family tradition, and what can be established about his identity, family, career, public role, and descendants?  
**Status:** Source-audited baseline. Several important genealogical claims remain unresolved.

## Executive finding

The man can be identified with high confidence as **Josiah Howlet**, born **6 July 1707 in Newton, Massachusetts**, son of **Michael/Michail Howlet and Experience**. He later appears in **Killingly, Connecticut** records under variants including **Hulet, Hulett, Hulit, Hulitt, and Hewlett**. His wife’s given name was **Lydia**, and Killingly town records directly document at least eleven children born to “Josiah & Lydia/Lidea/Lidia/Leada” between 1731/2 and 1758.

The specific title **“Captain” is not currently substantiated for this Josiah**. Broad exact-name searches across military, militia, naval, local-history, and genealogical material produced no commission, muster roll, vessel command, officer list, or contemporary record styling him Captain. A 1735 Killingly petition identifies him simply as **“Josiah Hewlett”**; the same historical narrative uses explicit Captain titles for other men. That is suggestive, not proof that he never held rank.

Two genealogical points require correction or caution:

1. **Lydia’s maiden name is unresolved.** The frequently copied identification “Lydia Morse” is problematic. The well-documented Lydia Morse, daughter of Peter Morse and Priscilla Carpenter of Woodstock, died in 1737, while Josiah and Lydia continued having children through 1758. That particular Lydia Morse therefore cannot be Josiah’s wife.
2. **Experience’s maiden identity is unresolved.** Josiah’s birth record proves his mother’s given name was Experience. Traditional Newton-family genealogies identify her as Experience Newton, but a 1711 probate file for Daniel MacKey reportedly names a daughter “Experience Howlet.” Until the original marriage and probate documents are compared, “Experience Newton” versus “Experience Mackey/MacKey” should remain an open problem.

A widely copied death year of **1789** for Josiah has not been independently proved in this pass. A compiled Kingsbury genealogy reports that his sister Experience’s will, dated 7 June 1797, mentions her “eldest Brother, Josiah Hulet, of Killingly.” The 1790 Killingly census, however, has no Josiah Hulet as a household head. He may have been living in another household, or the later will wording may not establish survival. The safest current death statement is **death date unresolved; 1789 is an unproved secondary-tree claim**.

## 1. Identity & Background

### Name and variants

Records use several spellings:

- **Howlet** — Newton vital record
- **Hewlett** — 1735 Killingly petition as reproduced by Ellen D. Larned
- **Hulet / Hulett / Hulit / Hulitt** — Killingly town-record index

These variants should all be used in archive searches.

### Birth

The published Newton vital records give:

> “Josiah, s. Michail and Experience, July 6, 1707.”

This is the strongest identity anchor found.

**Conclusion:** born **6 July 1707**, Newton, Middlesex County, Province of Massachusetts Bay.

### Parents

**Father: Michael/Michail Howlet.** This is directly supported by Josiah’s Newton birth entry.

**Mother: Experience [maiden name unresolved].** Her given name is directly supported by the same record.

Traditional compiled genealogy identifies her as **Experience Newton**, daughter of John Newton and Elizabeth Larkin, and places a marriage to Michael Howlett in 1704. A competing line of evidence reports that Daniel MacKey’s 1711 Middlesex County probate file (no. 14477) names a daughter **“Experience Howlet.”** The original probate packet has not yet been inspected in this research pass.

Because Josiah was born in 1707, the competing theories require more than simply replacing “Newton” with “Mackey.” It is necessary to determine whether:
- the Newton identification is wrong,
- the Mackey reference concerns a different Michael/Experience couple,
- Michael had two wives named Experience,
- or later genealogists conflated distinct families.

**Current confidence:** father Michael = high; mother given name Experience = high; mother’s maiden surname = unresolved.

### Childhood and migration

Secondary family research places Michael Howlet’s family in the Killingly, Connecticut area by the 1710s. A direct Killingly record shows **Oliver, son of Michael & Experience, born 15 January 1718/19**, confirming the family was established there by that date.

Josiah therefore appears to have been born in Newton and raised primarily in northeastern Connecticut.

### Education and occupation

No school, apprenticeship, college, trade-license, or occupation record for the elder Josiah was located. Assertions that he inherited a family homestead are present in secondary genealogy but were not verified with deeds in this pass.

## 2. Marriage and Children

### Wife

Killingly town records repeatedly identify Josiah’s wife only as **Lydia**, with spelling variants Lidea, Lidia, and Leada.

No reliable marriage record or maiden surname was located.

### Why “Lydia Morse” is doubtful

Online trees often attach **Lydia Morse (born 1 February 1710/11)**, daughter of Peter Morse and Priscilla Carpenter of Woodstock, to Josiah. Published/transcribed Woodstock material, however, gives that Lydia a death in **October 1737**. Josiah’s Killingly records show children with Lydia born in 1738, 1740, 1743, 1745, 1748, 1751, 1755, and 1758.

Therefore **Peter and Priscilla Morse’s Lydia cannot be the same wife who continued bearing Josiah’s children through 1758**.

This does not prove that Josiah’s wife had no Morse ancestry; it rejects the common specific attachment unless a second Lydia Morse can be documented.

### Children directly visible in Killingly Barbour records

The Killingly Barbour index directly records at least these children of Josiah and Lydia:

| Child | Birth |
|---|---|
| Lydia | 18 Feb 1731/2 |
| Michael | 20 Apr 1734 |
| Obadiah | 14 May 1736 |
| Jacob | 23 Sep 1738 |
| Experience | 8 Nov 1740 |
| Josiah | 13 Mar 1743 |
| John | 10 May 1745 |
| Daniel | 11 May 1748 |
| Abigail | 19 Jun 1751 |
| Eunes | 31 Dec 1755 |
| Elim | 16 Jul 1758 |

Some secondary genealogies list an additional earlier Abigail, producing twelve children. That first Abigail is not visible in the Barbour index excerpt examined and remains unverified.

### Critical identity trap: the younger Josiah

The son **Josiah, born 13 March 1743**, married **Elizabeth Whitemore/Whittemore on 16 February 1769** in Killingly.

Ron Bernard’s article *Josiah Hulet — Early Sandisfield Settler* concerns this **younger Josiah**, who later settled in Sandisfield, Massachusetts and died in 1834. Sandisfield deeds, wife Elizabeth, and 19th-century records must not be attached to the 1707 father.

## 3. Public / Professional / Military Career

### Documented civic activity: 1735 petition

Ellen D. Larned’s 1874 *History of Windham County, Connecticut* reproduces a 1735 South Killingly petition. Residents sought permission to hire an orthodox minister for part of the year and relief from the ordinary ministerial tax because of the distance to the established meetinghouse.

Among the signers appears:

> “Josiah Hewlett”

This places the elder Josiah as an adult landholding/community participant in Killingly at roughly age 27–28.

### Was he a Captain?

**Current verdict: unsubstantiated.**

Research searched exact and variant forms including:
- “Captain Josiah Hulett”
- “Capt. Josiah Hulet”
- “Captain Josiah Hewlett”
- “Josiah Hulett Connecticut militia”
- “Josiah Hewlett French and Indian War”
- Revolutionary War and militia combinations

No source located in this pass identifies the 1707 Josiah as a commissioned officer, militia captain, ship captain, privateer commander, or naval officer.

Larned’s Killingly history is especially useful context: the author explicitly styles other local figures as **Captain William Throop** and **Captain Howe**, while the 1735 material calls the subject simply **Josiah Hewlett**. This is not proof of absence, because Josiah could theoretically have obtained a rank later, but it weakens the assumption that “Captain” was a known local title for him.

### Likely sources of conflation

Several different men can contaminate search results or online trees:

- **Josiah Hulet, born 1743**, the subject’s son, later of Sandisfield.
- **Daniel Hulet, born 1748**, another son; later genealogical material associates younger family members with Revolutionary service.
- **Captain Richard Hewlett**, a different colonial-era Hewlett whose French and Indian War orderly book survives.
- **Josiah Hulett Carter / Siah Carter**, a 19th-century USS *Monitor* sailor, completely unrelated to this colonial identity.
- Other later Huletts/Hulets styled Captain in military records.

No vessel name, command, award, disciplinary action, or military chain of command can presently be assigned to the 1707 Josiah without risking conflation.

## 4. Historical Significance

Josiah’s verified historical significance is **local rather than national**.

He belongs to the early Howlet/Hulet/Hulett population of Killingly and is directly documented as:
- a Newton-born member of the Michael-and-Experience family,
- an adult South Killingly petitioner in 1735,
- husband of Lydia,
- father of a large Killingly family whose descendants spread into Massachusetts and later New England/westward communities.

No evidence found supports a historical reputation as hero, villain, controversial commander, or prominent public officeholder. The surviving documentary profile is that of an early New England settler/family patriarch participating in local ecclesiastical and tax concerns.

## 5. Connections & Network

### Immediate family

- **Michael Howlet** — father
- **Experience [Newton? Mackey?]** — mother, maiden identity disputed
- **Oliver Hulet** — brother, directly recorded as son of Michael & Experience, born 1718/19
- **Experience Hulet Kingsbury** — sister, married Ebenezer Kingsbury; her 1797 will is an important later reference to Josiah
- **Daniel Hulet/Hewlet** — brother represented in Kingsbury-family probate/deed material
- **Lydia [unknown]** — wife
- At least eleven directly documented children, listed above

### Kingsbury connection

A compiled Kingsbury genealogy records:
- Experience Hulet marrying **Ebenezer Kingsbury** in Plainfield in 1743/44.
- Hulet family members participating in Kingsbury estate/deed transactions.
- Experience’s will dated **7 June 1797**, exhibited **6 December 1803**, mentioning her **“eldest Brother, Josiah Hulet, of Killingly”** and second brother Oliver.

This establishes a strong Hulet–Kingsbury kin network across Killingly and Plainfield.

### Community network

Josiah appears among a cluster of South Killingly residents petitioning colonial authorities in 1735. Those names provide a useful neighborhood/community set for future deed, road, tax, and church reconstruction.

## 6. Death and Late-Life Evidence

### The 1789 claim

WikiTree, Geni, and other online trees commonly give **1789** as Josiah’s death year. No original death entry, gravestone, probate file, or contemporaneous notice confirming that date was found in this pass.

A notable warning: the Kingsbury genealogy explicitly states that **Daniel Hulet**, Josiah’s brother, died in 1789 and gives Daniel’s probate details. The repeated 1789 date for Josiah therefore deserves scrutiny for possible family-tree propagation or conflation.

### 1790 census

The transcribed 1790 Killingly census lists Hulet households including **Allim, David, Mehitable, and Oliver**, but **no Josiah** as a head of household.

At age 83, Josiah could have lived in another person’s household, so absence as a named head does not prove death.

### 1797 will reference

Experience Kingsbury’s will was dated 7 June 1797 and is reported to mention her **“eldest Brother, Josiah Hulet, of Killingly.”**

This is important but should be interpreted cautiously. A will’s wording strongly suggests the testator understood the person as a current kin reference, but without the original probate image or corroborating record it does not conclusively prove Josiah was alive on that date.

**Current death statement:** exact death date unresolved. Treat “1789” as unverified; do not yet replace it with “after 1797” as a proven fact.

The family clearly generated extensive later Hulett/Hulet lines, but a verified chain from the 1707 Josiah to specific living descendants was not constructed in this pass. Living people should not be identified solely from unsourced public trees. A later descendant project can work generation-by-generation from documented children.

## 9. What We Still Don’t Know

1. **Where did the “Captain” title originate?** A family Bible, gravestone, DAR/SAR application, local manuscript, militia list, or later family history may be the source.
2. **What was Josiah’s exact death date and probate district?** Killingly/Plainfield probate and land records should be searched directly.
3. **Who was Lydia?** Her marriage record and maiden surname remain unidentified.
4. **Who exactly was Josiah’s mother?** The Experience Newton vs. Experience Mackey/MacKey problem needs original documents.
5. **What was Josiah’s occupation and property history?** Deed, tax, and estate records remain to be reconstructed.
6. **Did he serve in any militia capacity without being a Captain?** Negative web searches are not enough; printed Connecticut colonial muster rolls and manuscript militia records need direct inspection.
7. **Is the twelfth child real?** The alleged early Abigail needs an original or published town/church entry.
8. **Where was he buried?** No reliable gravestone/cemetery record has been located.
9. **Which descendant line connects to the present family tree in this repository?** The binary family-tree export needs extraction in a local environment capable of reading the archive.

## Research discipline

Online family trees were treated as **leads**, not as authorities. Where a tree conflicts with a published town record, probate transcription, or chronological constraints, the tree claim is flagged rather than silently harmonized. The “Captain” title remains a research hypothesis until a rank-bearing primary or near-primary source is found.
