This is useful as a hypothesis map, but two GEDCOM values are now strongly contradicted by external records.

## 2. Correction: 7 September 1709 belongs to Daniel, not Nathaniel

The published Newton, Massachusetts vital records identify:

- Josiah, son of Michail and Experience, born 6 July 1707
- Daniel, son of Michail and Experience, born 7 September 1709

Therefore, the GEDCOM assignment of the same 7 Sep 1709 date to Nathaniel is almost certainly a copied or merged-tree error.

Nathaniel is nevertheless a separate real person. The 1735 South Killingly petition lists **“Nathaniel and Josiah Hewlett”** together, proving both men existed as distinct adults by then. Killingly records also identify Rachel, daughter of Nathaniel and Susanna, born 12 Feb 1744/5.

**Revised status:**
- Daniel: b. 7 Sep 1709 Newton — strong
- Nathaniel: separate brother — strong
- Nathaniel birth date/place — unresolved

Sources:

## 3. Major correction: Josiah death 27 Aug 1769 is not supported and is probably wrong

The supplied GEDCOM gives the elder Josiah a death of **27 Aug 1769 at Killingly**. Exact-date searches produced no death, burial, probate, church, or local-history evidence for that date.

More importantly, the published Kingsbury genealogy, drawing on Plainfield probate, says that Josiah's sister Experience Kingsbury made a will dated 7 Jun 1797 which:

> mentions eldest Brother, Josiah Hulet, of Killingly

The same passage calls Oliver her second brother.

This does not substitute for seeing the original probate image, but it is much stronger than an uncited GEDCOM death field and seriously conflicts with a 1769 death.

**Current conclusion:** remove or quarantine 27 Aug 1769 as an unverified tree assertion. Do not treat it as established death evidence.

Source:
- Kingsbury genealogy scan: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f2/The_genealogy_of_the_descendants_of_Henry_Kingsbury_%28IA_genealogyofdesce00ki%29.pdf

## 4. Nathaniel and Josiah were neighbors/associates, not duplicates

The 1735 South Killingly petition is especially informative because the signers include **“Nathaniel and Josiah Hewlett”** together. It places the brothers in the same South Killingly settlement network and establishes that Nathaniel cannot simply be a duplicate Daniel or Josiah record.

It also strengthens the interpretation that the Hulett family was embedded in the South Killingly religious and landholding community by the mid-1730s.

Source:
- Ellen Larned / local-history transcription: https://ldsgenealogy.com/CT/books/History-of-Windham-County-Connecticut-Volume-I-1600-1760-part-38.htm
- Modern reproduction quoting Larned: https://linpub.blob.core.windows.net/pdf/2/b1f792c7-b11d-407d-912e-f41b3013b998.pdf

## 5. The "Captain" hypothesis remains unsubstantiated

A new round of exact-name searches across:
- militia language
- French and Indian War terms
- Connecticut officer references
- Revolutionary-era material
- local histories
- land/probate references

still produced no rank-bearing reference to the elder Josiah.

The 1735 source names him simply **Josiah Hewlett**, even though the same historical literature regularly preserves Captain/Major/Colonel titles for other men.

There is a plausible mechanism for later family-memory inflation:
- Josiah's descendants served in Revolutionary units
- Michael Hulet (1734-1825) has documented Revolutionary associations in later local sources
- Daniel and other Huletts are also associated with Revolutionary service
- later Hulet descendants included militia officers
- military company records naturally contain many actual captains under whom Huletts served

This makes rank migration across generations a credible hypothesis, but not yet a demonstrated fact.

**Status:** do not call the 1707 Josiah “Captain” in the evidence tree until a commission, muster, town record, military return, or similarly direct record does so.

External compiled data agrees on the relationship:

## 9. Davis -> Douglass bridge in Ohio

Multiple independent derivative sources point to children of William Davis and Huldah Hulet, including:

- Sarah Louisa Davis, b. 1808 Killingly
- Asher Austin Davis, b. 1810 Killingly
- Fannie Davis, b. 1812 Killingly
- Mary J. Davis, b. 1815
- Lorenzo Davis, later generation evidence gives him as their son

The particularly useful anchor is Richland County, Ohio marriage material. On **16 March 1830**:

- **Ebenezer Douglass married Sarah Davis**
- **James Douglass married Fanny Davis**

both in Sharon Township, both by Elder Carlton.

This appears to be a double-family connection: two Davis sisters marrying two Douglass men on the same date.

Source:
https://richland.ohgenweb.org/MAR-1823-1831-Henney.htm

A separate genealogy preview identifies Sarah Davis as born 1808 in Killingly, married Ebenezer Douglass in 1830 at Mansfield, Ohio, and names children Mary E. Douglass and Joseph M. Douglass.

Source:
https://www.mytrees.com/names/Married/1830/4/SARAH%2BDAVIS

This is a promising descendant migration line:
**Josiah Hulet -> Michael Hulet -> Huldah Hulet -> Sarah Louisa Davis -> Ebenezer Douglass family.**

It should NOT yet be assumed to be the same Douglass branch as Eleanor Douglass in the existing ancestry reports. The existing text reports searched so far do not mention Huldah Hulet, Sarah Louisa Davis, or Ebenezer Douglass.

## 11. Michael Howlett parentage remains unresolved

The GEDCOM reportedly points Michael to family @F926. Many online genealogies call his parents **John Howlett and Abigail Powell**.

This should not be accepted merely because @F926 says so.

Older and later Hulet researchers themselves acknowledge uncertainty about whether the Killingly Michael belongs to the Ipswich/Boston Howlett family. One older compiled genealogy says he “may have been a descendant of the Ipswich family,” which is appropriately cautious.

The F926 block is worth extracting because it tells us what the user's tree claims, but the next phase must separately prove:
- Michael's birth/baptism
- his parents
- continuity from Massachusetts to Killingly
- absence of same-name competing men

## 12. Experience, wife of Michael: surname still unresolved

The Newton birth record proves the mother's given name **Experience**.

The traditional **Experience Newton** identification remains widespread, but evidence reportedly exists in Daniel MacKey/Mackey's 1711 probate naming a daughter **Experience Howlet**.

Until the original probate and marriage records are compared, Experience's maiden surname must remain unresolved.

## 13. Lydia, wife of Josiah: surname still unresolved

Direct Killingly child records consistently identify the mother as Lydia/Lidea/Lidia/Leada but not by maiden surname.

A recent researcher likewise reports no marriage record and labels her Lydia Unknown.

The commonly copied Lydia Morse identification should not be restored without new proof.

## Bottom line

The new GEDCOM context is extremely useful as a map, but it also demonstrates why the tree needs source auditing. The strongest corrections from this pass are:

- **Daniel**, not Nathaniel, owns the 7 Sep 1709 Newton birth.
- Nathaniel is a distinct brother, independently visible with Josiah in 1735.
- Elder Josiah's **27 Aug 1769 death is unsupported and probably erroneous**.
- “Captain Josiah” still lacks any rank-bearing source.
- Huldah's spouse is very likely **William Davis**, and that branch appears to move into Ohio.
- The Huldah/William branch creates a plausible **Davis -> Douglass** bridge via the 1830 Richland County double marriages.
- Michael's parents, Experience's maiden surname, Lydia's maiden surname, Josiah's death, and Huldah's exact dates remain open until primary evidence is obtained.
